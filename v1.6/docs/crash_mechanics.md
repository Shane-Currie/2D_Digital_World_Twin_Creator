# Crash mechanics — stage one

The player wagon now transfers impact momentum to outdoor NPCs, NPRs and traffic cars. Faster relative closing speeds produce stronger shoves; vehicle mass, collision direction and the contact point influence the response. Head-on and side contacts push in different directions. Off-centre car contacts also produce turning motion, with some of the impulse going into rotation rather than straight-line movement.

This is a top-down gameplay approximation, not a certified vehicle crash simulation. Person, robot and vehicle masses and sliding/spin resistance are shared constants in `scripts/runtime/crashes/crash_settings.gd`. Map pixels are converted to metres using the loaded town's scale. No town-specific coordinates, map changes or LLM calls are required.

Swept sprite envelopes locate the first contact, including reverse and high-speed movement. Buildings, water and bridge/tunnel boundaries still take priority. Cruise cancels on impact; the wagon loses momentum rather than always stopping instantly. Struck actors slide and turn with friction, then attempt to continue from their displaced position. They do not teleport straight back to their old position. A blocked traffic recovery can use the existing safe jam-recovery fallback.

NPCs and NPRs can take a validated corner-to-corner detour around the stationary wagon, including when it is occupied but stopped. Routes check buildings, water, other walkers and road-crossing permission. They try the unobstructed side; if neither side is safe, they wait. They do not step off a bridge to pass a car. NPC traffic still yields to a parked wagon; general traffic overtaking/lane changing is outside this walking-detour stage.

No health reduction, injuries, asset damage, deformation or ragdolls have been added. Contact-to-contact momentum now passes through crowded ground sprites, using their masses; this is not a full rigid-body pile-up simulation. The wagon emits a `sprite_impact` event containing actor kind, closing speed and impulse for a later validated damage system. NPDs are not ground crash targets. Spin is bounded for stable gameplay; the player wagon retains its existing steering model.

## Crossing crowd correction

Walking clearance now matches physical NPC/NPR radii. A walker may advance its graph route from a nearby clear position if another character occupies the exact waypoint, without snapping into that character. Avoidance retains forward progress instead of circling a destination. Admitted walkers may sidestep inside the reserved crossing corridor, but not off bridge/tunnel sides. A knocked walker already in the road clears the crossing without waiting for a new green phase; new crossings still require safe admission.

`tools/tests/verify_intersection_crowds.gd` reproduces the earlier immovable group and occupied-waypoint orbit failures and verifies their fixes. Final read-only Albury checks passed 20 clear-ground impact probes with zero failed shoves and recorded 164 walkers moving in a 20-second sample. Peak committed-roadway stall was 0.067 seconds, with none remaining at the end. Existing crash, parked-car, crossing, walking, destination and traffic regressions passed. Extended gameplay remains user-tested.

Focused verification: `tools/tests/verify_crash_motion.gd` covers speed/mass/scale, momentum and energy, front/side/corner contacts and turning direction, reverse, layers, real wagon integration, slower displacement, recovery, static walls, blocked water, rotated NPC/NPR parked-car detours and opposing walkers passing beside the wagon. Existing player controls, traffic flow, crossing clearance/spawn, intersection buildings and walker/bubble tests also passed. A read-only Albury 30-second signal-flow diagnostic moved all 150 traffic cars and recorded 7,017 committed-junction samples without an admitted car stopping for a signal inside its junction. These are focused checks, not extended gameplay/performance results.
