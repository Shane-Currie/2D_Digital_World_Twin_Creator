# Interior Designer — first usable floor stage

Choose the building, then open an app-style button: **Floors**, **Entrances**, **Walls & rooms**, **Floor textures**, **Furniture**, **My furniture**, **NPC placement** or **Location notes**. **Back** returns to these buttons without resetting edits or zoom. The building selector and top floppy disk remain available; scroll the buttons on smaller windows. Save before switching main sections.

The Interior Designer lists the Creator building name when one has been saved, with the stable OSM feature ID beside it. Renaming a building never changes that ID. Use the always-visible floppy-disk save button at the top of the section; changing pages before saving reloads the last saved design.

Open a saved town and create at least one exterior entrance on a building in **Building Creator**. Then choose **Interior designer** in the sidebar.

1. Choose the building.
2. Select **Create blank ground floor**. Creator Studio converts the actual OSM footprint to local metres. Concave edges and mapped courtyard holes are retained.
3. Optionally select **Add upper floor**. Each new floor starts with the same footprint shape.
4. Choose a floor and set its size from 100% to 300%, then select **Apply floor size**. This enlarges the full outline proportionally; it does not turn the floor into a rectangle. A size above 100% is saved as a creator adjustment, not a claim about the real building.
5. Choose an exterior entrance and select **Place selected entry point**. Creator Studio switches to the ground floor; click inside the usable floor area.
6. Under **Draw walls and name rooms**, select **Draw a new internal wall**. Hold the left mouse button, drag to its finish and release, or click the two ends separately. A live line previews the wall; a green ring marks a snapped connection to the footprint or another wall. Snap targets use an 18-pixel tolerance adjusted for zoom (bounded to 0.12–2 metres); otherwise points use the 0.25-metre grid. Slight mouse jitter is aligned horizontally/vertically. Drawing stays active so another wall can be drawn immediately; right-click cancels. The full line must remain inside the usable floor.
   To adjust a saved wall, choose **Select / drag existing walls** or select it in the wall list. Drag the middle of the wall to move it, or drag a round endpoint handle to extend/reshape it and connect it to another wall. Release to apply the preview. Door IDs and lock states are preserved, with doorway positions kept proportional along the wall. A move that crosses furniture, an entrance or a saved storyline NPC, or makes its doorways too crowded, is rejected and leaves the original wall in place. Choose **Save interior layouts** to persist edits.
7. Choose a 0.7–3 metre doorway width and select **Add doorway — click any wall**. Click the visible wall where the door belongs; Creator Studio automatically selects that clicked wall rather than relying on the wall list. Select the saved doorway and use **Lock selected doorway** when required. Unlocked doors show green and locked doors show red. Walls and doorways can be removed from their lists.
8. Enter a room name such as Bedroom, Kitchen or Office, select **Place room name**, then click clear floor space inside that room. Room names label the creator's wall layout; they do not claim a surveyed real-world floor plan. During gameplay the current room name appears beside the building/floor heading at the top rather than being printed on the floor.
9. Under **Paint the floor**, choose Plain floor/eraser, Light oak, Dark walnut, Weathered grey or one of the town's uploaded textures. Select **Paint selected floor texture**, then hold the left mouse button and drag across the floor. Hold **Shift** and click once to fill the enclosed room under the pointer. Full internal wall segments—including their E-operated door positions—form room boundaries, so a fill does not leak through a doorway into the next room. Plain floor/eraser restores painted areas to the original neutral floor. Flooring is visual and never adds collision.
10. To add a personal floor texture, type its name, select **Upload my floor texture** and choose a PNG, JPEG or WebP up to 20 MB and 4096 pixels per edge. Creator Studio copies it into this town before it becomes paintable; the source file may then be moved or removed. A square, evenly lit texture gives the neatest repeat, but the upload does not need to be mathematically seamless.
11. Under **Place furniture**, filter the illustrated list by **Residential**, **Generic business**, **Office**, **Shop**, **Restaurant**, **Pub**, **My creations** or a creator-authored category. The search box matches item names, object types and categories. Choose an item and select **Place selected furniture**. Its preview follows the mouse. Right-click turns it 45°; hold right mouse and drag horizontally for finer rotation, or enter degrees in the Rotation box. Click clear floor space to place it. Select an existing item by clicking it or choosing it in the placed-furniture list, then use the same right-mouse controls to rotate it. Rotations and placements through walls, courtyards, entrances or other solid furniture are refused. Save the interior layout to retain changes.
12. Use the visible **− / Reset / +** controls and mouse wheel to zoom. Left-drag on open floor pans the map. Wall and floor-paint tools retain their left-drag editing gestures; middle-drag always pans, including while a tool is active. Zooming and panning change only the view, never the saved metre coordinates.
13. To add personal furniture artwork, enter a display name, a plain object type such as `chair`, and the real width/depth. Choose one of the built-in usage categories or type a new category such as `Medical clinic`. Select **Upload my furniture artwork** and choose a PNG, JPEG or WebP. Creator Studio copies the picture into the town, adds a new custom category to both category selectors when needed, and automatically gives every placed copy a solid collision rectangle using those metre dimensions. Every upload also remains available under **My creations**.
14. In **NPC placement**, choose **Storyline NPC**, **Trader NPC**, **Generic NPC** or **NPR (robot)**, optionally supply a name, choose a compatible human/robot persona and select **Place NPC — click a clear floor position**. Click to stage the character on this floor; blocked placements show a red X. The character appears immediately and can be dragged. Save the interior to keep it. Placed generic characters currently stand at their saved points, rather than roaming indoors. Alternatively, **Select and copy interior location** copies a clear point's building, floor and X/Y metres for the separate NPC creator menus.
15. In **Location notes**, upload an optional UTF-8 `.txt` reference about this building. Import copies and saves the reference immediately; layout/NPC edits still need the top Save. Indoor characters receive their current building/floor context, and outdoor characters can also discuss relevant venue facts. Reopen Play test after changes. See [location notes](location_notes.md).
15. Select **Save interior layout**.

Paste the copied value into **NPCs and personas → Storyline NPCs**. It looks like `Interior: building=484843857; floor=ground_floor; x=8.50; y=4.25`. The creator checks that the building and floor still exist and that the point remains inside the usable floor rather than a wall/courtyard opening.

The canonical town file is `data/building_interiors.json`. Floors use stable IDs and remain attached to the stable OSM building ID. Painted areas use deterministic 0.5-metre cell keys and stable material IDs in each floor's optional `flooring` record. Each internal wall stores two metre points, thickness and zero or more doorway offsets/widths with an optional `locked` state; each room stores a stable ID, name and label position. Creator-imported furniture is stored in `data/interior_furniture_catalog.json`, while imported floor materials use `data/interior_floor_materials.json`; their copied pictures live in `assets/interiors/furniture/` and `assets/interiors/floors/`. Create/Rebuild preserves these files. Their formats are documented by the matching schemas and can be validated without an LLM.

The first library contains dining tables/chairs, sofas, single beds, wardrobes, kitchen counters, bathroom sinks, toilets, office desks, shop shelves, bar counters and bar stools. Shared objects can appear in several usage categories without duplicating their artwork or collision record. Their pictures appear directly in the filtered furniture drop-down. Each placed item stores its stable catalogue ID, name, object type, position, metre dimensions, rotation, colours, optional copied image path and collision setting in the selected floor's `furniture` array. The functionality-first built-in artwork includes item-specific top-down details—such as cushions, pillows, handles, worktop fittings, a monitor and shelf divisions—so furniture remains recognisable at normal gameplay scale.

In **Play test**, walk to the green exterior arrow and press **E**. The player transfers to the saved ground-floor arrival point and may walk only inside the footprint-shaped floor; courtyard holes, solid furniture and internal walls/doors remain blocked. Saved floor paint is rendered in the same metre cells used by Creator Studio and does not affect movement. Internal rooms separated by properly joined walls begin greyed out; the runtime also closes legacy wall gaps of up to one metre against footprint/other walls for room discovery and collision. Stand at a green internal door and press **E** to move safely to its opposite side; the room entered then becomes visible for the rest of that building visit. A red internal door is locked: E does not cross it and the player displays **The door is locked.** The player cannot accidentally walk through a door. The current room name appears in the top building/floor heading, not on the floor. Furniture uses the same saved picture, dimensions and rotation seen in Creator Studio. Clicking a furniture item temporarily displays a screen-safe speech bubble above the player's head, such as **It's a bed.** or **It's a sofa.** This identification is deterministic and does not use the local LLM or change the object; richer object interactions remain for later releases. The ground floor is fully included and uses the stable `ground_floor` ID. Press **M** indoors to open a fitted map of the current floor. Moving the mouse changes the map crosshair and its building, floor and local X/Y metre readout without moving the player. **Copy location** places the standard one-line `Interior: building=…; floor=…; x=…; y=…` text on the clipboard for Storyline NPC placement or another app. These location details are hidden during normal play. An interior storyline NPC appears at its exact saved point only in its assigned building/floor, so a character placed far from the entrance may require entering that room before it becomes visible. It supports the same **T** conversation flow. Return to the green interior marker and press **E** to exit at the same exterior arrow.

The editor does not infer room boundaries from OSM or claim surveyed plans. Room names are creator-placed labels within the layout made by its internal wall segments. Per-pixel collision from transparent artwork and richer object actions remain unimplemented. The earlier upper-floor travel limitation is superseded by v1.6 paired stairs: see [stairs and playable upper floors](interior_stairs.md). Use Interior Designer → Stairs to connect floors, then E in Play test; each floor retains its NPC placements, furniture and collision geometry.

## Direct furniture and doorway editing

Click an existing furniture item or coloured doorway marker directly on the floor preview, then drag and release to move it. Doorway picking takes priority over dragging its supporting wall; doors can move to another internal wall. Furniture keeps its artwork, dimensions, collision, rotation and stable ID. Doors keep their width and lock state; IDs are retained unless the destination wall already uses that ID.

Invalid furniture/door placement shows a red X over the attempted location and a short footer explanation, not a modal popup. Invalid moves leave the original object unchanged. Creation stays active after a blocked click so you can retry immediately. Previews use the same furniture/door rules as commits, including walls, courtyards, entry arrivals, other items and saved NPC clearance. Outside pointer positions are not silently clamped into the floor.

Select an item, press/hold **Shift**, and click clear positions to paste copies of that actual item, without reopening the library. Hold Shift when placing a library item to keep placing that choice. Copies receive new IDs and retain the source properties. Release Shift and place once more to finish, or press **Esc** to cancel the active tool. Right-click/drag still rotates furniture; drag empty floor to pan and use the wheel to zoom.

Saved interior storyline NPCs appear with their existing artwork and names on their assigned building/floor, including the ground floor. Creator Studio shows them even in rooms that would be undiscovered during gameplay, so creators can arrange furniture around them. The editor does not generate roaming population positions or require the local LLM for these previews.

Click a storyline NPC's sprite and drag it to a clear position on the current floor. Its feet follow the grabbed offset, and its name, persona and artwork stay unchanged. A red X marks positions outside the floor or blocked by a wall, furniture, courtyard or another storyline NPC; an invalid drop leaves the original position intact. Select the top floppy-disk **Save interior layout** button before leaving this section to save both the layout and moved NPC positions. Reopen Play test to load the changes. In **NPCs and personas**, the storyline list identifies interior characters by the building's display name and floor, for example **Kekie · The Pub / Ground floor**, rather than requiring you to recognise OSM IDs.

Exterior entry still uses the green arrow and **E**. A press made up to 0.4 seconds before reaching a linked entrance is remembered briefly, but the normal usable distance is unchanged. The request expires and cancels when opening the map, starting a conversation or entering a vehicle; it cannot repeatedly enter/exit from one press. Focused check: `tools/tests/verify_entry_press_grace.gd`, using the saved Albury pub without changing its data. This timing improvement does not establish the cause of every reported failed entry.
